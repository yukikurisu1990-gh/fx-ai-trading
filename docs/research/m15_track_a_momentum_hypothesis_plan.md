## 1. The reversal family is closed

`MULTI_DAY_REVERSAL_FAILED_SUPPLEMENTAL_HISTORY_REPLICATION` stands as the Track A
research conclusion, and this round records the operational consequence:

**`MULTI_DAY_REVERSAL_FAMILY_DROPPED_FROM_ACTIVE_EXPLORATORY_RESEARCH`.**

That is a resource decision, not a claim about the world. It does not say a
multi-day reversal does not exist; it says further optimisation of this family is
not worth the effort given `lb480_h480_z1.0` returned −577.3 net and −426.5
**gross** over `2023-04-26 … 2025-04-24`, with 1 of 20 pairs net-positive, 0 of 9
neighbourhood cells positive on either measure, 0 of 20 leave-one-pair-out and
0 of 8 leave-one-currency-out removals turning it positive, and a mean IC of
`+2.35%` against the development window's `−25.07%`.

## 2. Where this hypothesis came from, stated plainly

The supplemental IC came out **positive**, so the mirror of a failed rule is
arithmetically attractive. That is the whole provenance, and it is a bad one:
this hypothesis was generated by looking at the data that refuted its opposite.

**`POST_HOC_EXPLORATORY_HYPOTHESIS`.** The supplemental result is **not**
evidence for momentum and is not cited as such anywhere in this round. It is the
reason the question was asked, which is a different thing entirely. `+2.35%` is
also small in absolute terms and the round that produced it measured its own
overlapping-window bias at `−1.47%` on that span — so even as a *hint* it is
thin.

The only thing that can make this hypothesis interesting is data that has not
been looked at, which is why §4 exists.

## 3. The candidate, frozen here

**`lookback = 480` M15 bars, `hold = 480`, `entry_z = 1.0`, direction =
momentum.** Long what rose over the past 480 bars, short what fell — the exact
mirror of the reversal candidate.

**Implemented as a negation, not a copy.** `momentum._signal` returns
`-round2._signal(...)`. Every gate in the reversal signal is sign-symmetric —
negation commutes with `where`, `ffill` and `fillna(0.0)`, and the rollover block
tests `decided != decided.shift(1)`, which is invariant under negation — so the
negation of the finished series is exactly the negation of the raw position, and
nothing else changes. A copy of the function with `-1.0` and `1.0` swapped would
be the same thing today and free to drift tomorrow; `round2.py` is also left
byte-identical, which is what the parameter freeze rests on.

**No new indicator.** Same z-score window, same rebalance grid, same 8-phase
averaging, same rollover block, same `engine.evaluate`, same cost model.

## 7. What will be measured

Primary, on the fresh span: gross / cost / net pips per pair, Sharpe-like, max
drawdown, closed trades, win rate, expectancy, turnover, IC, pair breadth, and
chronological stability across coarse blocks. Split three ways: all `PAIRS_20`,
the six JPY pairs, the fourteen non-JPY.

**Tail dependence is mandatory**, because the reversal round's magnitude turned
out to rest on a handful of days in both periods: the contribution of the top
1/3/5/10 days and the worst 1/3/5/10, and the net after removing each. Removal is
a diagnostic and never becomes part of the rule.

Cost sensitivity at ×1.00, ×1.25, ×1.50, ×2.00, ×3.00 on the **unchanged**
`EXPLORATORY_ASSUMPTION` model — per-side `(observed spread + 0.5) / 2` on
mid-based returns. No new cost model.

Pair and currency robustness: per-pair net, leave-one-pair-out,
leave-one-currency-out, JPY versus non-JPY. If one currency bloc carries the
result, that is stated.
