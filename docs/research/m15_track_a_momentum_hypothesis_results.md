## 2. Provenance — why this was a weak hypothesis before any data was read

Momentum was proposed because the previous round's supplemental IC came out
positive. That is a hypothesis generated by looking at the data that refuted its
opposite, so it is `POST_HOC_EXPLORATORY_HYPOTHESIS`, and the `+2.35%` that
suggested it is **not** cited as evidence for it anywhere. It is worth adding
what an audit measured: that `+2.35%` is itself inside its own null band
(p ≈ 0.34). The hypothesis was built on a number that was never established.

The mirror identity was also clear before the read. Momentum and reversal hold
opposite positions of the same size at the same times, so turnover — and
therefore **cost** — is identical, and only gross negates:

```
gross_momentum = −gross_reversal      cost_momentum = cost_reversal
net_momentum   = −gross_reversal − cost_reversal    (never −net_reversal)
```

Both sides pay the spread. Inverting a losing rule cannot produce a winning one;
it produces a rule that must beat the spread from the other side. Checked, not
assumed: the two grosses sum to **0.0** and both carry cost **152.4**.

## 4. Scope: a third route, and a hole closed in all three

`bars._assert_span` and `supplemental.assert_supplemental_span` were **not
widened**. The momentum route is a third guard whose upper bound *is*
`supplemental.SUPPLEMENTAL_START_UTC`, so the three windows partition the
archive:

| window | route | state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `2021-04-26 … 2023-04-25` | momentum | read this round |
| `2023-04-26 … 2025-04-24` | supplemental | seen |
| `2025-04-25 … 2025-12-28` | development | seen |
| `2025-12-29 … 2026-02-28` | — | `EXPLORATORY_OOS_SLICE`, forbidden |
| `2026-03-01 … 2026-04-24` | — | dead window, forbidden |
| `2026-04-25 …` | — | forward epoch, forbidden |

An audit probed **every one of the 3,649 UTC dates** in the archive against all
three guards: no date admitted by two routes, none admitted by the wrong one,
and no span ending at or after `2025-12-29` admitted by any.

**A bypass was found and is now closed in all three.** Each guard validated the
*shape* of its bounds with `utc_date` and then compared **the caller's object**.
A `str` subclass overriding `__lt__` and `__ge__` to answer "no" to both bound
checks was therefore admitted, after which the reader decoded the entire
ten-year archive — the OOS slice, the dead window and the forward epoch
included. Reproduced on all three routes. They now compare the parsed
`datetime.date` values, which no caller can poison; for genuine strings the
behaviour is unchanged, because at fixed width string order and date order
coincide. The defect existed at base master and was inherited, not introduced.
Both halves of each guard have to be reverted for the hole to reopen, which is
why the test overrides both operators.

**No forbidden row was read.** Every parquet in all three caches was measured:
momentum `2021-04-26 00:00 … 2023-04-25 23:45`, supplemental
`2023-04-26 … 2025-04-24 23:45`, development `2025-04-25 … 2025-12-28 23:45`.
Zero bleed.

Nothing under `scripts/m15_track_a/` was touched: `implementation_surface()`
re-derives to 32 files and `implementation_fingerprint()` to `e147542a…`,
matching the recorded grant, and the WP5 reader-freedom pin passes.

## 5. The candidate, and how the mirror is implemented

`lookback = 480`, `hold = 480`, `entry_z = 1.0`, direction momentum — frozen at
`c0ba1cd`, **83 seconds before the first parquet was written**, with `lookback`
and `hold` read from `round2.CENTRE` rather than restated.

`momentum.signal` returns **`-round2._signal(...)`**. Every gate in the reversal
signal is sign-symmetric, so this is exactly the mirror. An audit tried to break
it across 432 configurations — three path types, eight seeds, two lengths, all
four ATR buckets, five holds, five thresholds — against a re-implementation that
negates the raw position at source and then runs every gate: **zero mismatches**.

The candidate is **standardised** momentum: long when the 480-bar move is large
*relative to its own trailing distribution*, not merely positive.

`round2.py`, `engine.py`, `bars.py` are unchanged in the machinery they carry.
`momentum_replication.evaluate_config` takes the signal as an argument because
the frozen one hard-wires it; a test hands it `round2._signal` and asserts it
reproduces `round2.evaluate_config` across all shared fields and the daily series
on **ragged** frames — an audit showed the first fixture gave every frame the
same index, under which the Round 1 row-alignment bug is invisible.

## 7. Detection power, against named alternatives

Power at the *observed* effect is a monotone restatement of the p-value and is
not reported as evidence. Two alternatives are named instead. **Neither was
pre-registered** — the plan named none, which is itself a defect — so both are
post-hoc and labelled so.

| alternative | total | power to detect it | p(observed vs it) |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| the motivating rate (supplemental momentum, scaled to 624 days) | +275.7 | **0.124** | 0.034 |
| the development window's reversal magnitude, scaled | +771.5 | 0.613 | 0.0004 |

The first row is the one that matters, because it is the effect the hypothesis
actually predicted, and **this study had a 12% chance of detecting it.** The
programme's own precedent is the supplemental round, which earned a *failed
replication* verdict with about 76% power against a named alternative. This
round is nowhere near that bar.

## 9. The IC, against its own null

Long-horizon past-vs-forward IC on overlapping windows is mechanically negative
under a random walk, and the bias scales with `lookback / n`. The first version
of this document compared ICs across spans of different length without measuring
it.

The null permutes the **rows of the aligned twenty-pair panel once**, so
contemporaneous moves stay contemporaneous. Two earlier constructions of mine
under-preserved that dependence and understated the null's spread; the module
now reports `effective_independent_pairs` beside the result as a self-check —
**4.64**, consistent with the 5–6 this programme has measured in every round. A
value near twenty would mean the null had decorrelated the panel and every z
computed against it was inflated.

| | value |
| --- | ---: |
| observed mean IC | −5.85% |
| null mean (the bias) | −1.48% |
| null sd | 3.60% |
| null 95% band | **[−8.45%, +5.88%]** |
| **p (two-sided)** | **0.233** |
| bias-adjusted IC | −4.37% |
| pairs with positive IC | 2/20 |
| null breadth | 8.61, 95% [1, 16] |
| **p (breadth, one-sided low)** | **0.053** |

The IC is inside its band. Breadth is the closest thing to a signal in this round
and it does not clear 0.05.

## 10. Pair, currency and tail robustness — and what these numbers cannot show

**Leave-one-pair-out: 0 of 20 positive** (−491.4 … −403.8). **Leave-one-currency-out:
0 of 8 positive** (−588.4 … −316.0). **Neighbourhood: 0 of 9 positive on net and
0 of 9 on gross** (−645.1 … −337.8).

These three are reported because the plan requires them, but an audit showed they
are **arithmetically forced, not three independent corroborations**. For any
leave-one-pair-out to turn positive, a single pair would have to carry net below
the pooled sum of −9,028.2; the worst is EUR_AUD at −1,356.2. The nine cells have
a cross-cell sd of 96.6 against a per-cell SE of 344.4 — about 92% correlated,
i.e. roughly one observation, not nine. Once the pooled point estimate is that
negative, all three outcomes follow.

**Tails.** 624 trading days, total −451.4.

| --- | ---: | ---: |

## 12. What this establishes, and what it does not

It does **not** establish that momentum fails. The interval spans zero (p = 0.187),
the IC is inside its null band (p = 0.233), and the study had 12% power against
the effect the hypothesis predicted. Calling that a failed replication would
claim a precision this span does not have.

It does **not** establish that momentum works. Nothing here is positive, at any
level.

It does **not** reopen the reversal family. Reversal is +146.7 on this span, but
at p = 0.667 — equally indistinguishable from zero. Recording that both
directions are null on the same 730 days is the single clearest fact this round
produced, and it is the reason
`MULTI_DAY_REVERSAL_FAMILY_DROPPED_FROM_ACTIVE_EXPLORATORY_RESEARCH` stands.

**B is not "keep looking".** Three consecutive spans have now produced nothing
separable from noise in either direction, at a scale where the cost model charges
about 150 pips per pair per 730 days against gross of a few hundred. The
operational conclusion is identical to C's: **no further optimisation of this
family, in either direction, and no new round without a new instruction.**

No GO, no edge claim, no Formal Confirmation, no OOS or forward data touched, no
broker or production claim, and no optimisation started from this result. The
supplemental IC being positive did not license this round's hypothesis, and this
round's result licenses nothing either.

## 13. Why the first version said C, and why it was wrong

Recorded because the reasoning failed in a way worth keeping.

The first version argued C from four things. Three carry no information: "IC
positive in only 1 of 8 blocks" is the null's own expectation; "0 of 9, 0 of 20,
0 of 8" is forced by the pooled estimate; "the sign alternates" rests on one
already-known, selection-confounded contrast. The fourth — that the sign is
negative at every level — is true and remains true, but a uniformly negative
point estimate with an interval spanning zero is what an underpowered null
result looks like.

It also reported no interval, no p-value and no named alternative anywhere, and
led its power section with observed power, which the underlying module's own
docstring calls "near useless for reading a null result".

One correction was mine to make twice over. My first permutation null aligned
pairs by row position, my second keyed on timestamps but reordered into each
pair's own index space; both decorrelated the panel and shrank the null's spread,
which would have put the observed IC *outside* its band. Two review roles
produced different numbers, the disagreement was resolved on the construction
rather than on the preferred answer, and the self-check that would have caught
it — effective independent pairs — is now computed and reported.
