## 1. The answer

Momentum fails on data no round had looked at. Over `2021-04-26 … 2023-04-25`
the frozen candidate returns **−451.4 pips per pair net** and **−299.0 gross**,
before any cost is charged. **0 of 9** neighbourhood cells are positive on net or
gross, **0 of 20** leave-one-pair-out and **0 of 8** leave-one-currency-out
removals turn it positive, and the mean IC is **−5.85%** — the *reversal*
direction, with only 2 of 20 pairs showing a positive IC.

And the finding that matters more than either: **the sign alternates by period.**

| span | order | mean IC | momentum net | reversal net |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| `2021-04-26 … 2023-04-25` **fresh** | earliest | **−5.85%** | **−451.4** | +146.7 |
| `2023-04-26 … 2025-04-24` *(seen)* | middle | +2.35% | +275.7 | −577.3 |
| `2025-04-25 … 2025-12-28` *(seen)* | latest | −25.07% | −386.0 | +262.1 |

Three adjacent periods, sign **−, +, −**. Neither direction replicates, and both
lose over the 1,460 days combined: momentum **−561.7**, reversal **−168.5**. The
multi-day effect at this scale does not have a stable sign, which is a stronger
statement than "momentum does not work" and it applies to the family, not to one
rule.

Only the first row is replication evidence. The other two are
`EXPLORATORY_SEEN_DATA`, shown for contrast and labelled at every appearance.

## 2. Provenance — why this was always a weak hypothesis

Momentum was proposed because the reversal round's supplemental IC came out
positive. That is a hypothesis generated by looking at the data that refuted its
opposite, so it is `POST_HOC_EXPLORATORY_HYPOTHESIS`, and the `+2.35%` that
suggested it is **not** cited as evidence for it anywhere in this document. The
fresh span is the only test, and it is the one that failed.

Note also what the mirror identity implies, which was clear before any data was
read: momentum and reversal hold opposite positions of the same size at the same
times, so their turnover and therefore their **cost is identical**, and only
gross negates.

```
gross_momentum = −gross_reversal      cost_momentum = cost_reversal
net_momentum   = −gross_reversal − cost_reversal    (never −net_reversal)
```

Both sides pay the spread. Inverting a losing rule does not produce a winning
one; it produces a rule that must beat the spread from the other side. On the
fresh span it did not: gross was already −299.0.

Checked, not assumed: reversal gross +299.0 and momentum gross −299.0 sum to
**0.0**, and both carry cost **152.4**.

No existing grant was widened, and nothing under `scripts/m15_track_a/` was

## 5. The candidate, and how the mirror is implemented

`lookback = 480`, `hold = 480`, `entry_z = 1.0`, direction momentum — frozen at
`c0ba1cd` before the read, with `lookback` and `hold` taken from `round2.CENTRE`
rather than restated.

`momentum.signal` returns **`-round2._signal(...)`**. Every gate in the reversal
signal is sign-symmetric — negation commutes with `where`, `ffill` and
`fillna(0.0)`, and the rollover block tests `decided != decided.shift(1)`, which
is invariant under negation — so this is exactly the mirror and nothing else
differs. A sign-swapped copy would be identical today and free to drift tomorrow.
A test asserts `momentum.signal == -round2._signal` bar for bar across four
configurations including both ATR buckets, and asserts the two are flat at the
same bars.

The candidate is **standardised** momentum: long when the 480-bar move is large
*relative to its own trailing distribution*, not merely positive. That is the
mirror of what was frozen, and it is what "relatively" means.

`round2.py`, `engine.py`, `bars.py` are unchanged. `momentum_replication.evaluate_config`
takes the signal as an argument because the frozen one hard-wires it; a test
hands it `round2._signal` and asserts it reproduces `round2.evaluate_config`
across all 20 reported fields and the daily series exactly.

## 9. Tail dependence

624 trading days, total −451.4.

| days removed | contribution | net after removal |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| best 1 / 3 / 5 / 10 | +49.2 / +139.2 / +219.3 / +378.3 | −500.6 / −590.6 / −670.7 / **−829.8** |
| worst 1 / 3 / 5 / 10 | −66.0 / −178.5 / −263.7 / −450.6 | −385.4 / −272.9 / −187.7 / **−0.8** |

**Removing the ten worst days of 624 erases essentially the entire loss**
(−451.4 → −0.8). The result is as tail-driven as the reversal rounds were, in
the same direction: a handful of days decides the sign. This is a genuine
qualification on the *magnitude*, and it is why §11 does not claim a reliable
negative effect. Removal is a diagnostic and is not part of any rule.

## 11. What this establishes, and what it does not

It **does** establish that the momentum hypothesis failed its one independent
test. The span was unread, the candidate was frozen before the read, the sign is
wrong on gross before any cost, and nothing in the neighbourhood or in either
bloc rescues it.

It does **not** establish a reliable negative momentum effect in 2021–2023. Ten
days of 624 carry the loss, 4 of 8 blocks are net positive, and two-sided power
at the observed effect is only 0.26 (effect sd 344.4, MDE 964.9). The
classification is about the **failure to replicate**, which is robust, not about
the magnitude, which is not.

It does **not** reopen the reversal family. Reversal is +146.7 on this span —
worth stating because hiding it would be dishonest and because it is where the
alternating sign shows up — but at **+0.235 pips/pair/day** against −0.925 on the
supplemental span and +1.236 on the development window, that is not a rehabilitation.
`MULTI_DAY_REVERSAL_FAMILY_DROPPED_FROM_ACTIVE_EXPLORATORY_RESEARCH` stands, and
this round strengthens rather than weakens it: a family whose sign alternates
across three consecutive periods is exactly what "not worth further optimisation"
describes.

**No new optimisation is started from this result.** No GO, no edge claim, no
Formal Confirmation, no OOS or forward data touched, no broker or production
claim.
